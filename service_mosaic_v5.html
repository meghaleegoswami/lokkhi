What you can ask a user directly, by hand, with no live connection to a bank or broker — and what stays out of reach until you cross into Tier 1.

Not legal advice. This is a product-design map, not a compliance sign-off — built from the Tier 0–3 framework from earlier sessions (Tier 0: no registration · Tier 1: FSIBO / Electronic Settlement Agency registration for aggregation · Tier 2: FIEA investment-advisory registration · Tier 3: full investment-management licensing). Every "grey" item below needs a real read from Japanese fintech counsel before launch — the line between "user hands you a document" and "you're functionally aggregating their accounts" is a facts-and-circumstances judgment, not a bright statute.

Generally Tier 0

The user actively types, photographs, or uploads something themselves, in a single, user-initiated action. You never hold their bank credentials, never poll an account on a schedule, and never act on their behalf. You're a note-taking and calculating tool, not an access point.

Pushes you toward Tier 1

You store credentials or an OAuth token and pull data on an ongoing basis without the user re-initiating each time; you present recurring uploads as a "connected" account; or you take any action on an account (transfer, cancellation) rather than just displaying what the user told you.

Making Tier 0 input feel effortless

Camera before keyboard

Anywhere a physical document exists — payslip, receipt, policy — default to the camera. OCR does the typing; the user just confirms.

Chat, not forms

One question at a time in a conversational flow reads as a text exchange, not paperwork — the same total data, far less perceived friction.

Estimates over exactness

Sliders and "roughly" toggles for numbers where precision doesn't matter yet. You can always refine later; don't demand precision up front.

Progressive profiling

Ask the minimum to deliver first value. Unlock deeper questions as engagement and trust build, not all at signup.

OCR-then-confirm

Extract everything you can from a photo automatically, then let the user tap "looks right" instead of retyping it.

Ask once, remember always

Never re-ask something already given. Surface it for editing only when the user chooses to update it.

Tap over type

Icon-based category taps, emoji mood scales, and preset chips beat free text wherever the answer set is finite.

Explain the "why"

A one-line reason before each ask ("so we can show your rough tax bracket") measurably lowers drop-off versus an unexplained field.

User pays a recurring flat or tiered fee for ongoing access to the product itself — no asset management, no marketplace cut sitting underneath it.
Regulatory dependency
Tier 0 in almost every case here — you're charging for software and insight, not a financial service. Facet is the exception: its "subscription" is a wrapper around licensed human advisory, so it required Tier 2 registration from day one, not as an upgrade path.
Start here
Chargeable at MVP with zero registration, as long as the tool delivers standalone value without needing live account data to justify the price.

Freemium / free trial→ Paid individual tier→ Household / family tier→ Employer benefit upsell (crosses into B2B)

2. AUM Feetier 2/3 from day one
Definition
A percentage of the money you manage, typically ~0.5–1%/year.
Regulatory dependency
Requires investment-advisory or investment-management registration before you can legally touch a single yen. There is no "start free, add AUM fees later" path within this model alone — you either launch fully licensed, or this model doesn't exist for you yet.
Start here
Not startable at Tier 0. This is the model that forces capital-raising and compliance work before anything is monetizable.
Maturation path
Full license secured→ Launch fully licensed (no MVP shortcut)→ Scale via distribution — bank white-labeling or brand→ Absorption into a bank's distribution (majority stake)

3. Free Tool → Funnel to a Licensed Servicetier 0 + tier 2/3
Definition
A genuinely free (not just a free trial) product that builds trust and a large user base, monetized entirely downstream by converting a fraction of users into a paid, licensed offering.
Regulatory dependency
The free layer is Tier 0. The paid layer is whatever tier the eventual service requires — usually Tier 2/3 for advisory. You need both halves built or partnered; the free tool alone never monetizes on its own.
Start here
Launch the free half immediately. The monetizing half has to exist — built in-house or via partnership — before the funnel produces any revenue.
Maturation path
Free dashboard (Tier 0)→ User crosses an asset/engagement threshold→ Warm handoff to fee-based advisory (Tier 2/3)

4. Transaction / Referral Cuttier 0
Definition

Regulatory dependency

6. B2B / B2B2Ctier 0 or tier 1
Definition
The paying customer is an institution — an employer, or another fintech — not the end consumer.
Regulatory dependency
Varies by sub-type. Selling content/tools to employers as a benefit stays Tier 0 — the real bottleneck is the enterprise sales cycle, not regulation. Selling your data-aggregation infrastructure to other fintechs requires Tier 1 registration, because you're the one actually touching live account data at scale.
Start here
The employer-benefit sub-path is chargeable immediately; the infrastructure-API sub-path needs Tier 1 registration in place before you can sell it.
Maturation path
Consumer Tier 0 app builds usage data→ Pivot/expand to employer benefit or B2B API→ B2B revenue eclipses original consumer product

7. Lending Margin (Interest Spread)tier 2+ from day one
Definition
You lend money — directly, or via your own credit line — and earn the spread between what you charge borrowers and your own cost of capital.
Regulatory dependency
Requires Money Lending Business registration (or equivalent) before you can extend a single yen of credit — arguably the heaviest capital-plus-compliance load of any model here, since you also need real balance-sheet capital to lend, not just a license.
Start here
Not startable at Tier 0, and not really startable lean at all. This model wants venture debt or balance-sheet capital behind it from day one.
Maturation path
There isn't a "start light, add later" path→ Tally tried the closest version and it's part of why it shut down in 2024

8. Card / Interchangepartner-dependent
Definition
You issue a debit or prepaid card, via a banking-as-a-service partner, and earn a small cut of each transaction's interchange fee.
Regulatory dependency
You typically don't need to hold a banking license yourself — a partner bank or BaaS provider holds the license and issues the card under their charter — but you do need a real card-issuing partnership agreement, with its own long lead time and diligence bar.
Start here
Not a pure Tier 0 launch, but lighter than becoming a lender yourself — a partnership dependency rather than a registration one.
Maturation path
Core app builds engagement (Tier 0)→ Card partnership negotiated→ Card launches as a secondary revenue layer
